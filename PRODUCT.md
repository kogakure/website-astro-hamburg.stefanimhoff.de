# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Exploring Hamburg is for Hamburg residents, visitors, and urban walkers who want to understand the city beyond its landmarks. They arrive to browse photographed walks, choose a district, and follow one person's street-level record of that place.

## Product Purpose

The site documents Stefan Imhoff's long-running project to walk Hamburg district by district, as completely as possible, with a camera and map. Success means that the archive remains easy to explore, the photographs and writing remain the focus, and a visitor leaves with a more specific sense of Hamburg's neighborhoods.

## Positioning

A first-person photographic atlas of Hamburg built by walking the city street by street.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary action: choose and read a district walk.
- Secondary action: understand the project through its introduction and aggregate walk record.
- The line to remember: “Street by Street, District by District.”
- Belief ladder: Hamburg is more than its familiar center; each district rewards close observation; these walks provide a credible, personal record; the archive is worth continuing through.
- Proof on hand: 20 published district walks, 837 in-article image references, 280 documented kilometers, and 3,884 minutes of walking.

## Brand Personality

Observant, grounded, maritime. The voice is personal and matter-of-fact rather than promotional. The experience should evoke curiosity, orientation, and the quiet satisfaction of discovering a city on foot.

## Anti-references

Do not resemble a generic travel blog, tourism campaign, luxury magazine, or nautical theme park. Avoid anchors, ship wheels, rope borders, glassmorphism, interchangeable card grids, faux-vintage decoration, and the Japanese Ma visual language of stefanimhoff.de. The personal site is an architectural reference, not a visual template.

## Design Principles

1. Let Hamburg's photographs carry the atmosphere.
2. Make the archive feel explored, not consumed.
3. Use maritime and cartographic cues as structure, never costume.
4. Preserve the specificity and sequence of every walk.
5. Keep orientation, reading comfort, and accessibility ahead of ornament.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Support keyboard-only navigation, visible focus, semantic landmarks, descriptive alternatives for meaningful imagery and embeds, strong text contrast, and reduced-motion preferences. Light and dark themes must both remain legible, and long-form district pages must not overflow or become tiring to read on narrow screens.
